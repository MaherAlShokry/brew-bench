# ☕ The Brew Bench

A coffee field guide and dial-in tool that works like an app on your phone. Set your grinder, brewer and water and see roughly what lands in the cup before you pour. It also has recipes, a brew timer, and a guide to where coffee grows and where each variety came from.

## Features

On a phone the app has five tabs: **Brew** (the dial-in), **Recipes**, **Beans** (scan a bag, your bean gallery and yearly top 5), **Explore** (origins, varieties, processes, seed to cup, techniques, the flavour wheel, coffee words, history and championships, with a word of the day) and **Settings** (your profile, with an optional account by email or Google; make it yours: light or dark, eight coffee colour themes from House roast to Cascara, Kenya and Geisha, six logos for the top bar and browser tab, text size, motion and which page the app opens on; then your tools, and **Export my data / Import a backup**, a single file with everything you've saved, to keep safe or move to another phone). On a computer every section sits in the tab bar.

| Section | What it does |

| **Dial-in** | Where you brew. Pick a recipe (or dial in freely), your coffee dose and the cup you want (Balanced, Sweeter, Brighter, More body, Cleaner), plus the coffee in front of you (roast, process, variety, days since roast). Grind, water, ratio, bloom and agitation are tuned for it, the steps are rebuilt for your dose and target (Kasuya's 4:6 is restructured with his own levers: the first-pour split for sweetness against acidity, the number of later pours for strength), and every change is explained. Move any slider yourself and it stays, with a Re-tune button. The estimated cup profile updates live with concrete fixes ("try ZP6 Special 5.3"), and the brew timer follows your adapted steps |

The coffee's name and roastery are picked up too (and can be edited): the roastery is found wherever it sits on the bag, from a known roaster's name, "Roasted by …", a logo line or the web address, so each roaster gets credit on the scan. Labels in English, Spanish and Portuguese are understood, small misreads are corrected, and a photo taken sideways or upside down is turned the right way. ## Install on your phone

**Opening scene:** opening the Android app or the home-screen app (and the website, once per visit) plays a short scene in your vibe's colours that follows the logo you picked: the Dripper pours a V60 into a cup; the Bean tumbles in green and roasts brown, with sparks at first crack; the Espresso cup fills from a shot; the Latte's heart blooms where the milk lands; the Cherries ripen from green to red on a swaying stem; the Moka heats on a flame and puffs steam; the Gooseneck tips forward and pours over a V60 on its server, which fills as the coffee drips through; the Hand grinder takes its beans and its crank goes round (behind the body and back) as the catch cup fills. Each has its own line under the name, and its own shapes drifting behind. About 2 seconds; a tap skips it, and it's off with reduced motion. Android's own launch screen uses the same background, so there's no white flash.

**Android app (APK):** open the [latest APK](https://github.com/MaherAlShokry/brew-bench/releases/latest/download/brew-bench.apk) on your phone and tap it to install. The first time, Android asks you to allow installs from your browser or file manager. A new APK is built and published on the [Releases](https://github.com/MaherAlShokry/brew-bench/releases) page every time `main` changes, and the app offers it: when it opens (at most every 6 hours) it checks the latest release and shows **A new version is ready** with an Update button, and the top of **Settings** shows the app's version with *Check for updates* (and *Update to …* when a newer one is out). The website has no such card: it updates itself. Once the app is on Google Play, Play updates it instead. In the app, sheets and menus slide up and away, screens slide the way you are going, and the phone's back gesture closes the open sheet or steps back a screen, and only leaves the app from the Brew home.

**Straight from the website:** open the live site, then:
- **iPhone (Safari):** tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** tap ⋮, then **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.

It opens full-screen like a native app and works offline after the first visit.

### Website hosting (Firebase Hosting)

The website lives at **https://brew-bench.web.app/**, on Firebase Hosting in the same Firebase project as sign-in and sync. The old address, https://maheralshokry.github.io/brew-bench/, still works. It shows a page that sends visitors on to the new address, keeping any shared link. Someone with coffees saved at the old address can bring them across first, because each address keeps its own storage. Signed-in people also get everything back through sync. The old address also keeps serving `events.json` (older apps read it) and the privacy pages.

Until the secret below is set, nothing changes: GitHub Pages serves the whole site at the old address, and links point there.

One-time setup:

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/), open the project, then **Build → Hosting → Get started**. Click through the steps; there's nothing to install.
2. Create a deploy key. In the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/serviceaccounts?project=brew-bench), click **Create service account**. Name it `github-deploy` and give it these roles:
   - **Firebase Hosting Admin**
   - **Firebase Authentication Admin**, so PR previews can sign in
   - **API Keys Viewer**
   - **Cloud Run Viewer**
   - **Service Usage Consumer**, so the deploy can check that Hosting is on

   Open it, go to **Keys → Add key → Create new key → JSON**, and download the file.
3. In GitHub, under **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions**, add a secret named `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_BREW_BENCH` and paste the whole JSON file as its value. Then delete the downloaded file.
4. Under **Actions → Deploy the website → Run workflow**, run it once. It deploys to Firebase and switches the old address to the forwarding page.
5. In the [Google Cloud credentials](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials?project=brew-bench), open the OAuth web client and add `https://brew-bench.web.app` under **Authorized JavaScript origins**, so Google sign-in works there. If you restricted the API key to websites, add `https://brew-bench.web.app/*` to it too.
6. In the Play Console, change the privacy policy and account deletion links to the new address.

GitHub Pages keeps serving the `gh-pages` branch (**Settings → Pages**). Don't edit that branch by hand; it's rebuilt on every deploy.

**Optional: a permanent signing key for the APK.** Without one, CI signs each APK with a throwaway debug key, so installing a newer version may ask you to uninstall the old one first. To sign every build with the same key, create one on your computer:

```bash
keytool -genkeypair -keystore brewbench.keystore -alias brewbench -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000
base64 -w0 brewbench.keystore   # macOS: base64 -i brewbench.keystore
```

Then add four secrets under **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions**: `BB_KEYSTORE_BASE64` (the base64 output), `BB_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `BB_KEY_ALIAS` (`brewbench`) and `BB_KEY_PASSWORD`. Keep the keystore file somewhere safe and never commit it.

## Shared brew log (Firebase)

Friends can log brews and share recipes together: each person joins the same shared log (a code like `ABCD-2345`, or an invite link) and everyone's entries and recipes appear live on everyone's phone, with the name of whoever added them. Entries made offline sync when the phone reconnects.

The log is stored in a free [Firebase Realtime Database](https://firebase.google.com/docs/database). One-time setup:

1. Go to the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/), click **Create a project** (Google Analytics can be off).
2. In the project, open **Build → Realtime Database → Create database**, pick a location, and start in **locked mode**.
3. Open the **Rules** tab, replace the rules with the ones below, and click **Publish**.
4. Copy the database URL shown at the top (like `https://your-project-default-rtdb.firebaseio.com`) and put it in `FIREBASE_DB_URL` near the top of the shared-log section of `src/app.js`, then push.

```json
{
  "rules": {
    ".read": false,
    ".write": false,
    "groups": {
      "$code": {
        ".read": "$code.matches(/^[A-Z0-9]{4}-[A-Z0-9]{4}$/)",
        "log": {
          "$id": {
            ".write": "$code.matches(/^[A-Z0-9]{4}-[A-Z0-9]{4}$/)",
            ".validate": "newData.hasChildren(['id', 'ts'])"
          }
        },
        "recipes": {
          "$id": {
            ".write": "$code.matches(/^[A-Z0-9]{4}-[A-Z0-9]{4}$/)",
            ".validate": "newData.hasChildren(['id', 'name', 'b'])"
          }
        }
      }
    },
    "profiles": {
      "$uid": {
        ".read": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid",
        ".write": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid",
        ".validate": "newData.hasChild('nick')"
      }
    },
    "users": {
      "$uid": {
        ".read": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid",
        ".write": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid"
      }
    },
    "userimg": {
      "$uid": {
        ".read": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid",
        ".write": "auth != null && auth.uid === $uid"
      }
    }
  }
}
```

The rules stop anyone from listing groups; a group can only be read or written by someone who has its code (about a trillion possible codes). Anyone you give the code or invite link to can read, add and delete entries, so share it only with the people you brew with.

## Your profile and signing in (Firebase Authentication)

Your face sits in the top bar (on a computer, at the end of the tab bar); tap it for your profile: a nickname, your name, a few words about you, a photo or a symbol on a colour, a coffee role from how you like your coffee ("Florals chaser", "Ferment explorer"), your favourite origin and brewer, where you brew, and your coffees, brews, origins and trophies counted. Your nickname signs your shared brews and heads your Beans page. The profile is saved on the device straight away, no account needed.

With an account you can **sign in with email** (create an account, confirm it from the email, reset a forgotten password) **or Google** (on the website), and **everything syncs** between your phones and the website: your coffees with their diaries, brews, recipes, scans and photos, your Top 5, trophies and favourites, your gear and calibration, and the app's look. Each item is merged on its own, so a diary entry written on the website and a brew logged on the phone both survive. Changes go up a few seconds after you make them and come in when the app opens or you come back to it. You can sign out, or delete your account with everything synced to it, from the profile. Sign-in tokens are never written into backup files.

One-time setup, in the same Firebase project as the shared log:

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/), open **Build → Authentication → Get started**. Under **Sign-in method**, turn on **Email/Password**, then **Google** (choose a support email). Open the Google provider again and copy the **Web client ID** under *Web SDK configuration*.
2. Under **Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains**, check that `brew-bench.web.app` is listed (Firebase adds it), and add `maheralshokry.github.io`.
3. Open **Project settings** (the gear) → **General**. Under *Your apps*, add a **Web app** if there isn't one, and copy its `apiKey`.
4. In `src/profile.js`, fill in `FB_AUTH` near the top: `{apiKey:'…', googleClientId:'…'}`. Make sure `FIREBASE_DB_URL` in `src/app.js` is set too (see the shared log above) and that the database rules include the `profiles` part, so each account can read and write only its own profile and data. Then push.

**Google sign-in in the Android app.** Google doesn't allow its web sign-in inside an app's web view, so the app uses Android's own Google account sheet instead. Google only shows that sheet to an app whose signing key it knows. One-time setup:

1. Add the four upload-key secrets (see *Publishing on Google Play*), so every build is signed with the same key.
2. In the [Google Cloud credentials](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials?project=brew-bench), choose **Create credentials → OAuth client ID → Android**. Enter the package name `io.github.maheralshokry.brewbench` and the upload key's SHA-1 fingerprint (`keytool -list -v -keystore brew-bench-upload.jks`). Nothing needs copying back: the app signs in with the existing Web client ID.
3. Once the app is on Google Play, Play re-signs it with its own key. Copy the **App signing key certificate** SHA-1 from Play Console → *Test and release → App integrity*, and add a second Android client with it.

Until then the button explains that Google sign-in isn't ready in that version of the app, and email works as before.

The API key is meant to be public; the database rules and the authorized domains are what protect the data.

## Editing

Most changes are data edits. Add a recipe to `RECIPES` in `src/data/brewing.js`, or an origin to `O` in `src/data/origins-varieties-history.js`. Then rebuild:

```bash
python3 tools/build.py          # builds index.html
python3 -m http.server 8080     # preview at http://localhost:8080
```

Adding a new origin country to the map also needs its ISO numeric code added in `tools/gen-map.mjs`, then:

```bash
npm install
node tools/gen-map.mjs
```

`python3 tools/build.py --artifact` builds a single-file version (without the manifest or service worker) for hosting as a Claude artifact.

To build the Android app yourself you need Node 22, JDK 21 and the Android SDK (Android Studio installs it):

```bash
npm install
npm run build:app                    # builds www/ and copies it into android/
cd android && ./gradlew assembleDebug   # APK lands in android/app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
```

`npm run icons` regenerates the Android launcher icons and splash screens from the PNGs in `assets/`, and `npm run vendor` refreshes the bundled font, jsPDF and label reader in `vendor/`.

The logo, a two-tone coffee bean split by its S-shaped crease, is drawn in `assets/logo.svg`. `assets/logo-light.svg` is the bean for light backgrounds, `assets/logo-foreground.svg` is the mark without its background, and `assets/logo-maskable.svg` has extra padding for rounded icon masks.

**Logo choices and the home-screen icon.** Settings → App logo offers the bean and seven more marks (espresso, latte art, dripper, cherries, moka pot, gooseneck, hand grinder). They're drawn once, in `src/data/logos.json` on a 24-unit grid with the vibe's colours, and used for the top bar and the browser tab. `python3 tools/gen-app-icons.py` turns the same file into the Android launcher icons (`res/drawable/ic_logo_*.xml`, adaptive icons in `res/mipmap-anydpi-v26/`, a fallback for Android 7 in `res/mipmap/`) and prints the `<activity-alias>` entries in `AndroidManifest.xml`. Each logo is one launcher entry and exactly one is on; `AppIconPlugin` (in the Android project) switches to the chosen one when the app goes to the background. When adding a logo, add it to `logos.json`, rerun the script, add its alias and its name to `AppIconPlugin.ICONS`.

**Vibes.** Settings → Appearance → Vibe picks a whole palette for light and dark (Roastery, Oat latte, Matcha café, Coffee blossom, Nordic roast, Desert mocha, Neon espresso, Caramel pop, Pure, Black coffee). Each is a set of colour tokens on `:root[data-vibe=…]` in `src/template.html`; the text shades (`--*-ink`) are worked out so small text reads at 4.6:1 or better on every background. The accent swatches below it change only the highlight colour.

## Notes on accuracy

The cup-profile estimator is a model built from brewing rules of thumb, not a measurement. Grind is its main lever: one recipe step (about 160 microns, roughly 7 ZP6 clicks) counts for more than water temperature, where 1 °C is worth about one ZP6 click, in line with brewing studies that find temperature a finer adjustment than grind. Grinder dials are placed on one shared size scale and checked against each maker's published ranges. Recipes credited to named people follow their published methods; recipes marked "classic" or "in the style of" are general techniques. History and variety facts were checked against sources, but some coffee history is legend, and those items are labelled as such. Corrections are welcome as issues or pull requests.
